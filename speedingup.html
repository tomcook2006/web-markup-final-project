Speeding up

After reaching the goal of going for a few runs you might be wanting to speed up or run longer distances.

Building distance

Start by slowly increasing how far you run rather than trying to run much further every week. You can start with one kilometer every 3 days to get your body used to the load that you are putting on it; once that feels comfortable you can try and do two and a half kilometers at a slower pace. This is where a running watch comes in handy as you can keep track of how fast you're running. After two and a half kilometers starts to feel comfortable you can keep moving up in distance - this also helps improve your speed on your shorter runs.

Equipment

After you have been for a few runs and want to improve, there are a few pieces of equipment that can make your training more comfortable and effective. A running watch can track your pace, distance, heart rate and other useful data. You don't even need the most expensive one but mainly focus on one that tracks your heart rate and has Global Positioning System">GPS. Also running shoes and lightweight weather appropriate clothing can help you feel more comfortable while you are running.

Gaining pace

Once you can comfortably run ten kilometers you can start working on specific training for improving your pace. Begin with short intervals at a quicker pace, followed by jogging to give yourself time to recover. For example, if you run three days a week you could do one long run in zone two. Then for your second run of the week you can do a tempo run. For a tempo run you want to first stretch and warm up and then run for 8 minutes and keep your heart rate at about 150-170 bpm, after that walk or jog for three minutes and then go again. On the last day you can do another long run in zone two.

What is zone two?

Zone two is 65-75% of your maximum heart rate. A guesstimate of your maximum heart rate is 220 minus your age.
